Why Italy

Benefits of Studying in Italy

Scholarships up to 100%

Regional (DSU) scholarships can waive tuition fully and add a yearly stipend, meals or housing for eligible students, based mainly on family income.

With or without IELTS

Several universities accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter or other English proof instead of IELTS. We match you to the ones that do.

Top-ranked public universities

Hundreds of English-taught bachelor's and master's programmes at historic, well-ranked public universities.

Work while you study

Students with a study permit can generally work part-time, helping cover living costs.

Live in the Schengen area

An Italian residence permit lets you travel across the Schengen countries during your studies.

Visa process handled

We guide you through Universitaly pre-enrolment, documentation and your embassy appointment.

FAQ

Study in Italy: Common Questions

Is studying in Italy really free?
Public universities in Italy charge low tuition linked to family income, and regional DSU scholarships can waive tuition completely and add a yearly stipend, meals or accommodation. It is free for students who qualify, so we check your family income documents and academic profile first.
Can I study in Italy without IELTS?
Yes, at some universities. Many accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter or an English proficiency certificate instead of IELTS. Requirements vary by university and programme, so we shortlist the ones that fit your profile.
When should I apply for the September 2027 intake?
Now. Many Italian universities open applications a year ahead and the visa stage (pre-enrolment through Universitaly and the embassy appointment) takes several months. Early applicants get more university and scholarship options.
Can I work in Italy as a student?
Students holding a study residence permit can generally work part-time during their studies. We explain the current rules during counselling.
Can I stay in Italy after graduation?
Graduates can apply to stay on in Italy to look for work or start a job, subject to the immigration rules in force at the time.
