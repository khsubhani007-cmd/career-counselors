Study in Ireland from Pakistan: Universities, Work Rights and Stamp 1G

Guide to studying in Ireland from Pakistan: why Ireland, January intake options, part-time work rules, the Stamp 1G post-study route and the visa process.

Career Counselors Team18 October 2026 · 6 min read
🇮🇪
In this article
  1. Why Ireland?
  2. Working while you study
  3. Stamp 1G: staying after graduation
  4. Visa process in brief

Ireland is an English-speaking EU country and the European home of many global technology, pharma and finance companies. That makes it a strong choice for students who want an EU degree and career options afterwards.

Why Ireland?

  • Teaching in English inside the European Union
  • Strong links between universities and industry
  • Post-study stay option for eligible graduates
  • Friendly, safe and welcoming country

Working while you study

Students with Stamp 2 permission can generally work part-time during term and full-time during official holiday periods. Rules can change, so we confirm the latest conditions during counselling.

Stamp 1G: staying after graduation

Under the Third Level Graduate Programme, eligible graduates can apply for Stamp 1G to stay in Ireland and look for work. How long you can stay depends on the level of your qualification.

Visa process in brief

  1. Get your offer letter and pay the required tuition deposit
  2. Arrange private medical insurance
  3. Prepare financial evidence and bank statements
  4. Submit your study visa application with all documents
💡

Irish visa officers look closely at your finances and your reasons for choosing the course. A clear, honest SOP makes a real difference.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a January intake in Ireland?
Yes. Some Irish universities and colleges offer a January or spring intake for selected programmes.
What is Stamp 1G?
Permission under Ireland's Third Level Graduate Programme that lets eligible graduates stay and look for work after their studies.

This guide is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Rules, fees and deadlines change. Always check the official university, scholarship and embassy sources, or ask our counselors for your case.
